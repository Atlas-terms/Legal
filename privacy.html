Privacy Policy

Atlas — your travel history, on your device.

Last updated: 28 August 2026

The short version. Atlas has no user account and no developer server. Your travel archive is stored on your iPhone. Nothing is sent to us — there is nowhere for it to go. We collect no analytics, show no advertising, and use no tracking of any kind.

If you turn on iCloud sync, your archive is copied to your own private iCloud account, which we cannot access. It is off until you turn it on.

Who this policy is from

Atlas is an iPhone application published by Anton Celnokov, based in Malta. Questions about this policy can be sent to atlas.appstore.application@gmail.com.

“Atlas”, “we” and “us” refer to that publisher. “You” means the person using the app.

What Atlas stores, and where

Atlas builds a travel archive from documents you choose to import — boarding passes, hotel confirmations, tickets, calendar files and similar. That archive contains whatever those documents contained: trips, journeys, places, dates, times, accommodation, travel companions you name, prices you record, notes you write, and the extracted text Atlas kept as evidence for each field.

All of it is stored locally on your device, in the app’s own storage. There is no Atlas account, no login, and no developer-operated server or database anywhere in the product.

What we never collect

  • No analytics or telemetry. Atlas contains no analytics SDK, no crash-reporting service, and no usage tracking.
  • No advertising and no advertising identifiers.
  • No third-party data services. Atlas does not send your documents or travel data to any external service for processing.
  • No passport numbers or national identity numbers. Atlas does not collect or store them, even when they appear on a document you import.
  • No contacts, no email, no photo-library scanning. Atlas can only see the individual files or photos you explicitly pick.

Documents you import

Documents are read entirely on your device. Text recognition, barcode reading and interpretation all run locally using Apple’s on-device frameworks. No document, image or extracted text is uploaded for processing.

Nothing becomes a saved record without your confirmation: Atlas shows you what it read, and where in the document it read it, before anything is written.

You control whether the original file is kept after import. When retention is off, only the travel data you approved is stored — the original file is not. You can also choose per import.

iCloud sync (optional)

Atlas can keep a copy of your archive in your own private iCloud account, so it appears on your other devices signed into the same Apple Account. This feature:

  • is off by default and starts only when you turn it on;
  • uses your private iCloud database, not ours — we have no access to it and cannot read, receive or recover its contents;
  • encrypts the travel content it stores, so that only your devices can read it;
  • can be turned off at any time, and the copy held in iCloud can be removed from within the app.

Your use of iCloud is governed by Apple’s own terms and privacy policy.

Sharing a trip with the people you travelled with

Atlas can give people who were on a trip a read-only copy of it, kept up to date as the trip changes. That copy is placed in your own iCloud account and shared with the people you name. Atlas has no server involved and cannot see it.

You choose, per trip, whether to include:

  • prices and booking references,
  • your own notes,
  • the text Atlas read from your documents.

All three start off. Your imported original documents are never included in a shared trip under any setting.

If you stop sharing, the people you shared with keep the copy they already have — updates simply stop. This is stated in the app before you share.

Sharing you initiate yourself

Atlas can produce share cards and PDF reports. These leave your device only when you send them, through the system share sheet, to a destination you choose. What happens to them afterwards is governed by whichever app or service you sent them to.

Permissions Atlas may ask for

  • Camera — only when you choose to photograph a document. The image is processed on device.
  • Photos and files — Atlas uses the system picker, so it receives only the items you select. It never reads your photo library.
  • Calendar — optional and off by default. Reading is limited to the calendars you tick, events stay on your device, and nothing becomes a travel record without your review. If you enable calendar export, Atlas writes only to a calendar it creates, named “Atlas Travel”. Your own calendars are never written to.
  • Face ID / passcode — optional, and only to lock the app.

Each permission is requested at the moment you use the feature that needs it, never during onboarding.

Apple services

Some parts of Atlas rely on Apple, and are governed by Apple’s privacy policy rather than this one:

  • Purchases are handled entirely by the App Store. Atlas never sees your payment details, and receives only whether a purchase is active.
  • Maps — map tiles and place search are provided by Apple Maps.
  • iCloud — if you turn on sync or share a trip.

Diagnostics

Atlas keeps a small local log of warnings and errors to help diagnose problems. It records counts and states rather than the contents of your archive — travel details, names, booking references and document text are deliberately excluded. The log stays on your device and is only ever shared if you export it yourself and send it to us.

Your control over your data

  • Export everything as JSON or CSV, at any time, free. This is deliberate: your archive should never be locked inside Atlas.
  • Correct or delete any record, at any time.
  • Delete everything from the device in one action.
  • Remove the iCloud copy separately, from within the app.

Because Atlas holds no data about you, there is nothing for us to disclose, correct or erase on request — the controls above are the complete set, and they are all in your hands. If you are in the EU or UK, this is how Atlas is designed to satisfy your rights of access, portability and erasure.

Children

Atlas is not directed at children and does not knowingly collect information from anyone. It has no account system and no data collection of any kind.

Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, the updated version will be published at this address with a new “last updated” date. Material changes affecting how your data is handled will also be described in the app’s release notes.
